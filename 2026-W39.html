Ho Chi Minh City office demand shifts as AI and GCCs change the rules

  • Ho Chi Minh City’s office market is being reshaped by AI, digitalisation, GCC growth and sustainability. According to OpenGov Asia, occupiers now want smarter, greener and more flexible workplaces rather than just central locations and lower rents. The shift is creating new opportunities for landlords, developers and the city’s expanding talent base.
  • Savills Vietnam has said the biggest structural change is the evolution of Global Capability Centres, or GCCs, from back-office support units into Strategic Capability Centres. These hubs are now handling technology, research and development, data management and global operations, which raises the bar for office design and building performance.

Vietnam ranks among top 10 most improved global real estate markets

  • According to the Global Real Estate Transparency Index (GRETI) 2026 report released by JLL on September 15, Vietnam currently ranks 50th out of 88 countries and territories surveyed, with a transparency score of 3.15, remaining in the "Semi-Transparent" category.
  • Notably, Vietnam is one of 10 markets with the strongest score improvements globally during the 2024-2026 survey, alongside Poland, Thailand, Australia, Qatar, South Korea, India, Saudi Arabia, Dubai, and Abu Dhabi.
  • The index evaluates 88 markets globally based on 256 criteria across six categories: Performance Measurement (25 per cent), Regulatory and Legal (23.5 per cent), Market Fundamentals (16.5 per cent), Transaction Process (15 per cent), Listed Vehicle Governance (10 per cent), and Sustainability (10 per cent).

Infrastructure · 06 of 8

Nikkei’s claim that "Britain is planning up to $2.5 billion in financing to help Ho Chi Minh City extend a metro line" is not just inaccurate—it is functionally useless for anyone tracking actual infrastructure development in Vietnam.

  • It Confuses a Credit Ceiling with Real Money An Export Credit Agency (ECA) capacity limit is a theoretical country ceiling, not a signed check or an approved loan. Reporting UKEF’s indicative $2.5B market envelope as "planned funding for a metro line" is like saying a bank pre-approved a mortgage for a house that hasn't even been designed yet.
  • It Ignores Tied-Financing Constraints UKEF facilities are tied loans. Without a matching commercial contract, that $2.5B remains purely on paper.
  • It Disregards Legal & Public Debt Realities Before any foreign ECA debt hits a Vietnamese infrastructure project, it must navigate the Law on Public Investment, national debt ceiling caps, and complex multi-jurisdictional approvals (HCMC - Đồng Nai).
Why it mattersIf financial journalism wants to remain relevant to project finance professionals, it needs to stop treating political soft-diplomacy announcements as real capital deployment.

Investors remain wary of Vietnam's resort property market

  • Vietnam's resort property market continues to face a mismatch between abundant supply and weak liquidity, with about 11,000 villas, resort townhouses/shophouses and condotels offered for primary sale in August but only 112 transactions recorded.
  • According to DKRA Consulting, primary supply across the three resort property segments – resort villas, resort townhouses/shophouses and condotels – stood at about 11,000 units in August, little changed from 11,004 units in July.
Why it mattersIn August, supply edged down to 2,400 units, while sales fell to just nine, down 64%, pushing the absorption rate to 0.4%.
